# Exchange

Exports of the design to files other programs read, and the DXF import (milestone 9): the plan drawing as DXF, SVG and PDF, the building as glTF, PNG renders, a DXF imported as linework to trace over, and the dialogs that run them from the project tab.

## What it does

- **`export.plan`** turns each requested level into a plan drawing (`planDrawing` in `packages/core/src/exchange/drawing.ts`: layered strokes, fills and texts in millimetres, from the same derived geometry the canvas draws) with the display units and rule limits from Settings, then writes it with one of the pure writers in core:
  - **DXF** (`drawingToDxf`): AutoCAD R2000, millimetres, one CAD layer per drawing layer, texts with their cap height (0.72 of the font size the SVG and PDF use) on their baseline, middle or top; poché and the stair's start dot as solid hatches; one file per level. Colours are AutoCAD colour indices per role (`DXF_COLORS` in `dxf.ts`): ink, connectors and labels 7 (it flips with the CAD background), poché and graphite (slab outline, dividers, fixtures) 8, the slab tint 9, rooms 75 (nearest to the sage), dimensions 165 (indigo), furniture and lights 35 (umber), warnings 42 (ochre). A DXF has no title block: model space is unitless and the sheet is the SVG's and the PDF's.
  - **SVG** (`drawingToSvg`): one self-contained document per level, on a paper sheet (A4, A3, A2, A1, Letter or Tabloid, portrait or landscape; A3 landscape by default) at 1:`scale`, in paper millimetres so it prints to scale, one `<g>` per drawing layer named after it, arcs as `A` commands, texts upright, in the print palette's inks (`PRINT_COLORS` in `packages/core/src/exchange/colors.ts`, the identity's light plan palette with ink for the lines; the sheet itself stays white), and a 90 × 24 mm title block in the bottom-right corner inside the margin: row 1 the product's name from `BRANDING.productName` in bold with a terracotta full stop (`#b9552f`, the block's only colour) and the project name right-aligned, row 2 the level, row 3 `Scale 1:N` (the scale the layout chose, also at `fit`) and the date (`YYYY-MM-DD`, today unless `date` is given); a 0.35 mm frame with one rule under row 1. The sheet's text uses `'Alegreya Sans', Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` and `'IBM Plex Mono', ui-monospace, Consolas, monospace`, so it reads in the identity's fonts where they are installed and falls back to Helvetica. The drawing area stops 4 mm above the block (`drawingArea`), so `fit` never puts the plan under it, and a drawing too large for its scale is clipped to that area.
  - **PDF** (`drawingToPdf`): one file with a page per level, a hand-written PDF 1.4 (no dependency): true vectors, arcs as Béziers, tints through an ExtGState alpha, page texts in the standard Helvetica, so in WinAnsi (Latin-1 and the Windows-1252 extras: `m²`, `€`, accented Latin letters; control characters become spaces and any other character `?`), the same title block (the product's name in Helvetica Bold, the full stop as its own terracotta text run, widths measured with the Helvetica and Helvetica Bold AFM tables) in the same print palette; the document's title (the project name) is UTF-16 and keeps every character; content streams compressed with node's `deflateSync`.
- **`export.building`** writes the building in 3D as one self-contained GLB (`buildingToGlb` in `packages/core/src/exchange/gltf.ts`): metres with y up as glTF requires, a root node named after the project, a node per level named after it with a node per kind of solid, a node per connector starting on it (`Stair`, `Elevator` ...) and a node per furniture item named after its model, placed by its 4 × 4 matrix. Colours are the 3D view's (materials' colour, roughness, metalness and opacity, paints, glass see-through and double-sided); patterns are written as their plain colour, lights are not written. Imported models are read from the project's `assets/` through `host.projects.readAsset` (registered files only) and embedded with their own materials' base colour and alpha, `alphaMode` (and `alphaCutoff`) and `doubleSided`, a mirrored node's triangles wound back to face outwards; one whose file is missing or unreadable, or that has no triangles to write, is left out and listed in `skipped`.
- **`export.image`** writes a PNG render: the window draws it the way `view.snapshot` does (main has no canvas; `requestSnapshot` from the plan module emits `view.snapshot.request` with the size, the theme and `transparent`, and waits up to 60 s for the hidden `view.snapshot.reply`), then the module checks the PNG's IHDR against the size asked for (`checkedPng`, with `readPngHeader` from `packages/core/src/exchange/png.ts`) and writes it. A plan in the light or dark palette, or with no paper at all; the building in 3D with the 3D view's options, its canvas cleared to alpha 0 without a sky when transparent. **4096 × 4096 in 3D:** the WebGL drawing buffer is capped by the GPU; when Chromium gives a smaller buffer than asked for, `renderBuildingToPng` throws and the command fails with `UNAVAILABLE` naming the size it could draw, and nothing is written; a smaller image is never returned (the IHDR check in main refuses one too). The smoke accepts either outcome at 4096 × 4096 and logs which.
- With several levels, DXF and SVG put the level's name before the extension (`Plan - Ground floor.svg`, numbered when two names clash); PDF writes the one path. Those derived files are not replaced silently: when any of them is there already, nothing is written and the command fails with `CONFLICT` naming them (`details.existing`, full paths) unless `overwrite` is true; the one path of a single file is always replaced (whoever named it, the save dialog included, chose it). `fit` (SVG and PDF) picks, per level, the largest of 1:20, 1:25, 1:50, 1:100, 1:200, 1:250, 1:500 and 1:1000 that fits inside the 10 mm margin above the title block. With a fixed scale a level may be larger than the sheet: it is still written, clipped in SVG and PDF to the drawing area (inside the margins, above the title block, which stays clear), and reported with `fits: false`. A long project or level name in the title block is shrunk to 70 % of its size, then cut short with `…`. Lengths and areas are written in the display units from Settings, or in `units`.
- After writing, the module announces `exchange.exported { projectId, format, files }` (`format` is `dxf`, `svg`, `pdf`, `glb` or `png`).
- Files are written with `writeFileAtomic` (the folder is created when missing). Paths must be absolute, and never inside the project library's folder (`INVALID_INPUT`; compared without case on Windows), which the library owns.
- **`import.dxf`** reads a DXF file (at most 200 MB, refused before reading; UTF-8, a BOM dropped; read as Latin-1 when it is not valid UTF-8, as older ANSI files are; a binary DXF or a file without sections is refused) with `readDxf` (`packages/core/src/exchange/dxf-read.ts`) and turns its model space into edits of one level with `dxfToOps` (`dxf-import.ts`), applied as one undoable transaction labelled `Import <file name>`; its `doc.patched` is the announcement. Each DXF layer that has something to import becomes an **interior** layer named `<layerPrefix><DXF layer>` (`DXF Walls`), after the level's layers; DXF layer names are compared without case, as DXF does (the table's spelling wins, else the first entity's), and a layer of that name in any case already on the level is reused (on a reused layer that is not interior, open lines become open shapes, since dividers need an interior layer). DXF layers that are off or frozen come in hidden. Lengths are scaled by `scale` (default from `$INSUNITS`: 1 unitless or millimetres, 25.4 inches, 304.8 feet, 10 cm, 1000 m and the rest of the DXF table; without `$INSUNITS`, 25.4 when `$MEASUREMENT` is 0, imperial, else 1) and moved by `origin` (mm), then rounded to 0.001 mm.
- **`import.dxf.probe`** reads a file the same way and touches nothing: its units (`$INSUNITS`, `$MEASUREMENT`), the scale an import would use, each layer with its entity count and whether it would be hidden, what would be skipped, the paper-space count and the extent. The import dialog shows it first.

| DXF entity | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| LINE | a separator (a two-point open path) |
| LWPOLYLINE, POLYLINE (2D; 3D flattened to XY) | closed: an unfilled shape; open: a separator; bulges as arc segments, widths ignored |
| ARC | an open unfilled shape with one arc segment, so it does not split rooms (a full turn: a circle) |
| CIRCLE | an unfilled closed shape of two half arcs |
| ELLIPSE | flattened (64 segments per full turn): an unfilled shape, closed when full, open (an elliptical arc) otherwise |
| SPLINE | evaluated from its control points and knots up to degree 3 (rational ones too), else through its fit points, then flattened like an ellipse |
| TEXT, MTEXT | a label: the text (MTEXT's formatting codes stripped, paragraphs joined by spaces, `%%d %%p %%c` as ° ± ⌀), its size the font size: the DXF height (the capitals', what the writer puts in group 40) divided by `CAP_HEIGHT` 0.72 and scaled (or `textHeight`), its rotation, its point moved to the text's estimated middle since labels are centred |
| INSERT, MINSERT | its block's entities with the reference's scale (a negative one mirrors), rotation and base point (layer 0 taking the reference's layer); nested references one level deep; arcs under a non-uniform scale flattened to elliptical ones; a MINSERT's columns × rows (70, 71) copies spaced by 44, 45 along its rotated axes (past 10,000 copies in all, nested arrays multiplied: skipped as `MINSERT (too many copies)`) |
| ATTRIB (after an INSERT with 66 = 1) | its value as a label where it stands, like a TEXT (vertical alignment from 74); an invisible one (flag 1) skipped as `ATTRIB (invisible)` |
| HATCH, DIMENSION, POINT, SOLID, 3DFACE, IMAGE, WIPEOUT, LEADER, mesh polylines and anything else | skipped and counted by type in `skipped`; also `INSERT (nested)` past one level, `INSERT (no block)`, `<TYPE> (degenerate)` under two distinct points and `<TYPE> (empty)` texts |
| paper space (code 67 = 1) | ignored, counted in the probe's `paperSpace` |

## UI (`ui/`)

- **Export…** next to the project tab's view switch (also `Export drawing, model or image…` in the palette) opens the Export dialog (`ui/export-dialog.ts`, a Signal Form in a `Dialogs.open` component dialog). The format comes first (PDF, SVG, DXF, glTF, PNG) and only its fields show:
  - PDF, SVG, DXF, glTF: the levels, the one on screen or all of them (the dialog says what that makes: one PDF with a page per level, one DXF or SVG per level named after it, one model of the level or of the building);
  - PDF, SVG: the page (A4 ... Tabloid), the orientation and the scale (fit to page, 1:50, 1:100, 1:200, 1:500);
  - glTF: include furniture, include finishes (both on);
  - PNG: plan (the level on screen) or 3D, the size (1600 × 1200, 2560 × 1920, 3840 × 2880 or custom, 64 to 4096 px a side, checked in the form), the theme (the app's current one, each time the dialog opens) and a transparent background. A 3D image is seen as the 3D view shows the building: its stored camera (azimuth, elevation), display (all, isolate, cutaway), explode and lighting (`view3d` in the project's view state), summed up under the choice.
  
  The dialog starts from the last answer in the session (but for the PNG theme). Like every `Dialogs.open` dialog, focus moves to its first field, Tab stays inside, Escape or a click outside cancels and focus goes back to the button that opened it. On Export the native save dialog (`app.pickSavePath`) asks where, with the format's filter and `<project name>.<ext>`; the command then runs as the UI and a toast says `Exported <n> file(s) to <folder>` (with `; <level> does not fit the sheet at 1:<n>` when a level is larger than the sheet at the chosen scale) with a **Show** action (the hidden, UI-only `app.revealPath`, which selects the file in Explorer or Finder); a failure shows the error in a toast. When a DXF or SVG of several levels would replace files that are there already, a confirm dialog lists them and **Replace** runs the export again with `overwrite`. Until the command returns, the toolbar's Export… is disabled and shows a spinner and `Exporting…`.
- **Import DXF…** next to it (and in the palette and the Layers panel's add menu) picks a file (`app.pickFile`, DXF filter), runs `import.dxf.probe` and shows the import dialog (`ui/import-dxf-dialog.ts`): the file's units, the scale (mm per drawing unit, from the units, editable), the extent at that scale in the display units, the layers with their entity counts to tick (all ticked; layers off or frozen in the file marked Hidden), what the import skips, the target level (the one on screen; from the Layers panel, the panel's level), the origin (mm) and the layer prefix (`DXF `). Import runs `import.dxf` (one undo step), shows the target level and says what came in: `Imported <n> shapes, <n> dividers and <n> labels on <n> layers`, with `(<n> skipped)` counting only the entities the import does not support (not the degenerate or empty ones it dropped). A file the probe refuses (not a DXF, too large) shows the error in a toast and no dialog. The Layers panel follows through `doc.patched`.

## Storage

Nothing in the library: the files go where `path` says, which may not be inside the library's folder. The project is only read.

## Agents

`export.plan` (**gated**: it writes outside the library, so MCP agents are refused with `MUTATION_BLOCKED` until the user allows mutations in Settings, and the in-app assistant asks first). Input: `path` (full file path), `format` (`dxf` | `svg` | `pdf`), `levelIds` (default every level, bottom to top), `scale` (1:N, default 100), `page` (default `A3`), `orientation` (default `landscape`), `fit`, `layers` (drawing layer kinds; default all), `title` (default true), `units` (any of Settings' unit fields, `system`, `metricUnit`, `imperialStyle`, `precision`, `fraction`; the rest from Settings; unknown fields refused), `overwrite` (default false: several level files that are there already fail with `CONFLICT` and `details.existing`). A path inside the project library is refused with `INVALID_INPUT`, as for the other two exports. Returns `{ format, files: [{ path, levelId?, bytes }], levels: [{ levelId, name, scale, fits }] }`; `fits` is false for a level larger than the SVG or PDF sheet at a fixed scale (always true for DXF and for `fit`).

`export.building` (**gated** likewise). Input: `path` (full file path), `levelIds` (default every level; a connector goes with the level it starts on), `includeItems` (default true), `includeFinishes` (default true). Returns `{ path, bytes, nodes, triangles, skipped }`.

`export.image` (**gated** likewise; needs the app window, like `view.snapshot`). Input: `path` (full file path), `levelId` (default the ground level), `view` (`plan` | `3d`, default `plan`), `width` and `height` (integers 64 to 4096, default 1600 × 1200), `azimuth` (default 225), `elevation` (default 30), `display` (`all` | `isolate` | `cutaway`), `explode` (0 to 1), `lighting` (`day` | `night`), `theme` (`light` | `dark`, default `light`), `transparent` (default false). Returns `{ path, bytes, width, height, view, levelId, levelName }` (not the image: `view.snapshot` is for looking). Fails with `UNAVAILABLE` when no window draws it within 60 s or it cannot be drawn that large, `NOT_FOUND` for an unknown level or nothing to draw, `INVALID_INPUT` for a relative path.

`import.dxf` (**allowed**: it is an ordinary undoable design edit and reading a file destroys nothing, so it is not mutating). Input: `path` (full file path), `levelId` (default the ground level), `scale` (mm per drawing unit; default from `$INSUNITS`, or `$MEASUREMENT` without it), `origin` (`[x, y]` mm, added after scaling), `layerPrefix` (default `DXF `), `layers` (DXF layer names, compared without case; all by default), `textHeight` (mm, for every label). Returns `{ levelId, scale, counts: { shapes, separators, labels, layers }, skipped, layers: [{ id, name, dxfLayer, created, visible }] }`. An agent then finds the drawing with `plan.query` by those layer ids and traces walls over it. `INVALID_INPUT` for a relative path, a file over 200 MB or a file that is not an ASCII DXF, `NOT_FOUND` for a missing file or an unknown level.

`import.dxf.probe` (allowed; global, it needs no project). Input: `path`. Returns `{ insUnits, measurement, units, scale, layers: [{ name, entities, color, hidden }], skipped, paperSpace, extents: { min, max } | null }` (extents in drawing units).

The smoke's duplex came from `SMOKE_KEEP=<dir> npm run smoke`, which copies the exchange section's exports out of the smoke's temporary data folder.

- **DXF** (8 files: 4 of the houses, the duplex's 2 levels, the metric and imperial room): `ezdxf.readfile` then `audit()`: 0 errors and 0 fixes in every file; `ezdxf.recover.readfile` needed no recovery (0 errors, 0 fixes). `$INSUNITS` 4, `$MEASUREMENT` 1, AC1015. The layer table is layer 0 plus the drawing's layers in order, and each layer's entity count equals its strokes, texts and hatched fills (the ground floor: 11 layers, 71 entities, e.g. Stairs and lifts 31, Doors and windows 8, Rooms 7); entity by entity in order, every TEXT's content and cap height (0.72 of the font size), every ARC and CIRCLE's centre and radius and every LWPOLYLINE bulge resolved with `ezdxf.math.bulge_to_arc` to the drawing's arc (centre and radius within 0.001 mm). The door swing is a single arc, so it is an ARC (r = 900 mm, the door's width, on the DASHED line type), not a bulge; the bulge checked is the shape's arc. `$EXTMIN`/`$EXTMAX` contain every entity but texts exactly; see the quirks for texts.
- **PDF** (10 files: 8 of the houses, the duplex at 1:100 on A4 and an agent's one-level PDF at `fit`): `PdfReader(strict=True)` opens each without an exception, a Python warning or a logged message (so no xref rebuild), and every xref offset points at its object; the page count is the level count; the media boxes are A3 landscape 1190.55 × 841.89 pt, A4 landscape 841.89 × 595.28 pt and A4 portrait 595.28 × 841.89 pt; `extract_text()` of each page holds the level's name, the room names and areas (`m²` decoded from WinAnsi), the project name, `Scale 1:<n>` with the scale the layout chose (1:50 and 1:25 at `fit` on A3) and the date; the fonts resolve to the standard Type1 Helvetica and Courier with WinAnsiEncoding. The A3 1:100 and the clipped A4 PDFs were also opened in Chromium's own PDF viewer (PDFium) and looked at.
- **GLB** (5 files: the two houses, the duplex, its upper level only, an agent's): 0 errors, 0 warnings, 0 infos, 0 hints. The root node is the project, its children the level nodes in order; the node count and the triangle count equal what `buildingToGlb` reported (drawing house 1042 triangles, 11 materials; glTF house 1282 triangles, 14 materials including `paint:#aabbcc` and the imported table's `top` and `base`; duplex 692 triangles, 6 materials); every material is used and no two share a name.
- **SVG** (9 files): well-formed XML (`xml.etree`), `width`/`height` in mm equal to the viewBox and to the sheet (A3 landscape 420 × 297, A4 portrait 210 × 297); loaded in a hidden Electron window: no parse error, no console message (only Electron's own unpackaged-app security warning), every text and path present, captured and looked at.
- **Found and fixed:** a level too large for the sheet at a fixed scale ran past the margins and under the title block in SVG and PDF (seen in the Chromium render of the smoke's `Too big.svg`), although this README said it was cut off at the margins. Both writers now clip the drawing to the drawing area (`clipPath` in SVG, `re W n` inside `q`/`Q` in PDF) when it does not fit, and draw the title block outside the clip; `svg.test.ts` and `pdf.test.ts` check it.
- **Reader quirks, not bugs:** `ezdxf.readfile` keeps R2000's `\U+00B2` escapes in TEXT as written (its `recover.readfile` decodes them, as AutoCAD does; the check decodes with `ezdxf.decode_dxf_unicode`). ezdxf adds the `Defpoints` layer to a document it loads. ezdxf's bounding box of a TEXT is its own font estimate, a descender below the baseline whatever the letters: for the middle-aligned 250 mm `Entrance` label it reaches 15 mm below the drawing's bounds, which use the text's em box, so `$EXTMIN` is 15 mm above ezdxf's estimate there (AutoCAD recomputes the extents on a zoom). The glTF-Validator's npm package has no executable.
- **Still open:** DXF import has been tried only with the app's own output and the snippets in `dxf-read.test.ts` and `dxf-import.test.ts`; no file from AutoCAD, LibreCAD or another program has been imported. The DXF export has been read by ezdxf only, not opened in AutoCAD or LibreCAD; the PDF was read by pypdf and shown by PDFium, not by Acrobat; the GLB was validated but not opened in Blender or another viewer.
