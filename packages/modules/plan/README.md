# Plan

Editing on a level: layers and entities, and batches.

- **Layers** belong to a level and have a kind: structure (walls, openings, columns, slabs, voids, shafts), interior (rooms, fixtures, electrical and plumbing points) or decor (furniture items, lights). Dimensions and labels go on any layer. Every level starts with one of each; more can be added. Layers can be shown or hidden, locked, faded (opacity) and reordered. Moving the opacity slider makes many small changes that merge into one undo step.
- **Entities** are validated against their schema (`packages/core/src/doc/schema.ts`) on every change, with lengths rounded to 0.001 mm and defaults filled in. Their geometry is the `Path`: vertices joined by lines, arcs (by bulge) or cubic Béziers, with fillet or chamfer corners stored as parameters. An opening sits in a wall on the same level, at a distance along its path; deleting the wall deletes its openings. Columns, shafts and voids can span several levels (`span.topLevelId`) and then show on every level they pass through, mezzanines included.
- **The plan view** is the main view of the project tab (a Plan | Section switcher sits above it): the active level drawn from above on a canvas by `@studio/viewport`'s `plan2d` (see `packages/viewport/README.md`), with an adaptive grid in display units. The toolbar switches the level (also PageUp/PageDown with the canvas focused; the levels panel and the section follow the same choice; the level and reference selects keep their width and the toolbar wraps onto a second row when the tab is narrow, instead of squeezing them), shows the level below or above faintly as a reference, and zooms (wheel at the cursor, buttons, Home or the fit button). Middle-drag or Space+drag pans. The footer shows the cursor in display units. Columns, rooms (a badge at the seed, or the drawn region), slabs, voids, dimensions and labels are drawn from their data; an agent's change appears on the canvas at once.
- **Walls** are drawn joined: each wall's body is derived from its reference line (`path`), thickness and alignment (the body centred on the line, or to its left or right), exactly for straight and arc walls, and the bodies of a level are united (Clipper2) together with corner fills where wall ends meet, so L corners close to their miter at any angle and thickness mix, T junctions and crossings merge, and curved walls keep their arcs. A wall is one entity per segment unless drawn as a single wall, which is how a run gets fillet or chamfer corners. Nothing of this is stored: `packages/core/src/geometry/walls.ts` derives it, and the plan view caches it by object identity (see `packages/viewport/README.md`).
- **The Wall tool** (`W`, or the toolbar): click the points of a run; every click adds a wall from the last point and shows the length while drawing; `A` makes the next segment an arc (click its end, then a point it passes through); clicking the first point closes the run; Enter ends it; Escape drops what is unfinished, then leaves the tool (`V` selects). The options bar sets thickness and height in any unit, which side of the line the body lies on, and whether the run becomes one entity.
- **Selecting and editing** (`V`, the default tool): click an entity to select it (the smallest thing under the pointer wins: a door before its wall, a wall before the room around it), Shift+click to add or remove, click empty floor to clear, Ctrl+A for everything on the level. Drag from empty floor for a marquee: to the right it is a window (what lies fully inside), to the left a crossing (what it touches, dashed). Drag a selected entity to move the selection (Ctrl drags a copy); walls move joined with their neighbours in the preview and one command is sent on release. Arrow keys nudge by 100 mm (Shift: 1 m), merged into one undo step. Delete deletes; Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V copy and paste at the cursor (a wall's openings come along, as one batch); Ctrl+D duplicates. `R` rotates: click the pivot, a reference point, then the new direction (Ctrl keeps a copy); `Shift+M` mirrors across two clicked points. Right-click opens a menu (duplicate, delete, select all, zoom to fit). Escape cancels what is in progress, then clears the selection. Entities on hidden layers are not drawn or hit; locked layers are drawn but cannot be selected from the canvas (commands and agents can still change them). An entity that vanishes while selected (an agent's delete, an undo) leaves the selection, and a drag whose entity vanishes is dropped.
- **Grips and corners:** a single selected entity shows its grips: squares at the vertices of its path (a wall's reference line, a region's outline and holes) and circles at the middle of each segment. Drag a vertex to move it; the ends of other walls that meet it come along (Alt detaches), as one change. Drag a segment's middle to curve it through the cursor (Alt adds a vertex there instead); double-click it to straighten. Click a vertex to select the corner: the Properties panel then edits its position, its segment (straight, or an arc by radius and direction) and its corner (sharp, rounded by radius, chamfered by two distances); `F` and `C` round or chamfer it from the keyboard, Delete removes it, Escape goes back to the entity. A rounded or chamfered corner between two straight edges shows a diamond grip on its arc or line: drag it along the corner's bisector to size the fillet or chamfer (a chamfer keeps the ratio of its two distances; the size stops where the edges run out), pull it onto the vertex to make the corner sharp, or type the size while dragging. Shapes, room dividers, walls and regions all have these grips and corners.
- **The Properties panel** (right column) shows the selected entity's fields in display units, typed in any unit (`30cm`, `11' 6"`): a wall's thickness, height (empty for the level's), which side of the line its body lies on, and its length; an opening's kind, width, height, sill, distance along the wall, hinge and swing side; a room's name and number; a label's text, height and rotation; a column's size and rotation; a slab's thickness; a dimension's offset; every entity's name. A field that is not a length turns red while typing and is put back on Enter. Several entities of one kind edit together (one change). Every edit carries the change number the panel saw, so an edit on stale data is refused and the panel shows who changed the entity meanwhile.
- **Stale edits:** edits addressed by index (a vertex, a field from a copy) may carry `baseSeq`, the change number the caller's copy had, and `epoch`; if the entity changed since, the command fails with `CONFLICT`, naming who changed it (you, the assistant, or an MCP agent by name), and nothing moves. The editor sends them with every grip and inspector edit.
- **Snapping:** while drawing, dragging or placing points, the cursor snaps within about 10 px to what is near, ranked endpoint, intersection, midpoint, centre (arcs, columns), perpendicular or tangent from the segment being drawn, the nearest point on a line, the extension of a line (with a dashed guide; two extensions crossing give an apparent intersection), and finally the grid. **Angle tracking** (on by default) works like CAD polar tracking: while a tool takes a direction from a last point (walls, dividers, polylines and shapes, dimensions, a dragged vertex, rotate's third click, the stair and ramp axes), the cursor within 3° of a ray (a quarter of the step when the step is finer, so the directions in between stay free) or within a few pixels of it, at one of the main angles (every 45°), at a multiple of the chosen step (5° to 45°), or parallel or perpendicular to the previous segment of the run or to the wall or line the run started on, is held on that ray: a dashed guide runs along it, a label gives the angle and the length, and the footer names it ("90°", "Perpendicular to wall", "Parallel to previous"). Tracking ranks below the points you aim at (an endpoint, an intersection, a midpoint, a centre, a perpendicular foot) but above a line merely under the cursor, and where the tracked ray crosses a nearby wall it snaps to that crossing. A length typed while tracked runs along the tracked ray. Alt suspends snapping and tracking; Shift locks the direction to 45° steps. Sources are wall reference lines and faces, opening jambs, column and region outlines and dimension lines. The footer and a marker on the canvas say what the cursor snapped to (a square for an endpoint, a triangle for a midpoint, a circle for a centre, a cross for an intersection, a right angle for a perpendicular). The magnet in the toolbar switches snapping off and on, the compass next to it switches angle tracking off and on (pressed while on; its tooltip gives the step), and the menu toggles each kind, angle tracking and its step, the grid's visibility and the grid snap spacing (typed in any unit). These settings, and the Wall tool's thickness and height, are kept in the global settings (`editor` in `config.json`), so they follow the user across projects.
- **Exact input:** while a tool waits for a point (drawing a wall, moving or reshaping a selection, placing a mirror axis), typing a digit (or `@`, `-`, `.`, `+`) opens an input at the cursor. Enter takes a length along the current direction from the last point (`3000`, `3.5m`, `11'6"`), a polar coordinate (`3000<45`), a relative offset (`@1000,500`) or an absolute position (`1000,500`); while rotating, an angle in degrees. Escape closes it; text that is not a coordinate is refused and stays to be corrected. The length and angle of the segment being drawn are shown next to it. Lengths and angles elsewhere are typed in the tool options and the Properties panel.
- **Doors and windows** (`D`, `N`): hover a wall and the opening previews where the cursor projects on it, moved into the nearest gap between the wall's ends and its other openings (red when the wall has no room); click to place it, or type a distance from the wall's start (negative: from its end). For doors, `F` flips the swing side and `H` the hinge while placing. Sizes come from the options bar and are remembered in the settings (door 900 × 2100, window 1200 × 1200 with a 900 sill by default). The opening is cut out of the drawn wall; a door shows its leaf and swing, a window its glazing lines. Click an opening to select it before its wall; drag it to slide along its wall (over another wall, it moves there); a door's hinge and swing grips, the right-click menu and the Properties panel flip its swing; arrow keys slide it. An opening that no longer fits (its wall was shortened, or another opening overlaps it) is drawn in the warning colour; milestone 5 lists it as a rule warning.
- **Rooms** (`R`): hover a space the walls enclose and it lights up with its area; click to make it a room. A detected room keeps a seed point: its outline, area and perimeter follow the space the walls, columns and room dividers enclose around the seed at any moment (net area, from the wall faces, with freestanding columns taken out), so walls can move and the room follows. If the walls open, the room stays but is marked "not enclosed" and loses its area until the seed sits in a closed space again (drag the seed grip, or close the walls); two seeds in one space mark the second as sharing it, counted once. A gap of under 2 mm (a wall snapped to another wall's face) still encloses. Rooms can also be drawn as regions. The plan writes each room's name, number and area at its label point; the Properties panel shows area, perimeter and the enclosure state. **Room dividers** (`S`) are lines where there is no wall (a kitchen off a living room); they split the space like a hair-thin wall. **The automatic slab** (`slab` with `source.k = 'auto'`) is the outline of every run of walls that encloses something, drawn as a faint floor. Every connector passing through a level's floor (a stair or ramp arriving there or passing a mezzanine, an elevator above its lowest stop) cuts its footprint out of that level's automatic slab and drawn slabs; nothing of this is stored, so moving a stair or changing a level's height moves the hole.
- **Shapes** (`P` polyline, `K` rectangle, `O` circle): any outline that means nothing to the building (a terrace, a pool, a counter, a sketch), on any layer. The polyline tool clicks the points; `A` makes the next segment an arc through a point; clicking the first point or `C` closes it, Enter ends it open, Escape drops it. The rectangle takes two opposite corners (or a typed `@width,height`), the circle its centre and a point on it (or a typed radius). A closed shape is filled unless the option says otherwise; its name is written at its centre, and the Properties panel shows its area and perimeter (an open shape's length). The options bar also says what the outline **makes**: a free shape, or a room, slab or void drawn as a region (always closed, outlines counter-clockwise), so the same gestures draw regions. Shapes are snapped to, selected anywhere inside when filled or along their line otherwise, and edited like every path (grips, corners, the inspector).
- **Dimensions** (`L`) come in five kinds, chosen in the options bar: aligned (the distance between two points, the line offset to one side), horizontal and vertical (the distance along one axis), angle (click the corner, then a point on each side; drawn as an arc whose radius is the offset) and radius (the centre, then a point on the arc). Click the points, then where the line goes (or type the distance; Enter puts it at the cursor). A point that snaps to a corner of a wall (its reference line or a face) or of a shape **ties** the dimension there: the end follows that wall or shape through every later edit, and the Properties panel says how many ends are tied. Deleting the wall or shape unties the dimension in the same change; moving a dimension without what it is tied to, copying it alone, or removing the tied corner unties it too, and an untied end keeps the point it showed. A vertex inserted before a tied corner shifts the tie along. Selected, a dimension shows grips at its points (drag one onto a corner to tie it, elsewhere to untie it) and at its line (drag to change the offset). The plan draws each dimension with extension lines, ticks and its reading in display units; `plan.dimensions` returns the readings for agents. **Labels** (`T`): click where the text goes, type it in the input that opens there, Enter places it with the text height from the options bar; the Properties panel edits text, height and rotation.
- **Connector and column tools:** the Stair tool (`Shift+S`) clicks the walk line: two clicks for a straight stair (the start and the top), three for an L or a winder (the corner between) and for a curve (the top, then a point the arc passes through), two for a U (the start and the direction of the first flight) or a spiral (the centre and the first step), whose walk lines are laid out from the rise and the rule limits (`stairAxisFrom` in `packages/core/src/geometry/connector-intent.ts`); the options bar gives the shape, width, the levels it joins (this floor and the one above unless chosen) and the turn, and the preview shows the treads with the riser count in the footer. The Ramp tool (`Shift+R`) clicks the axis and ends on Enter or a double click; the Ladder tool (`Shift+L`) clicks the foot, then the direction it leans towards; the Elevator tool (`E`) previews the shaft at the cursor and a click places it, stopping at every stacked level from the lower to the upper level chosen (shaft and car sizes in the options). The Column tool (`C`) places a rectangular or round column at the click, snapped like any point (to a wall face, a centre, an endpoint), with its size, rotation and an optional span up to a chosen level. Sizes are remembered in the settings; a column, shaft or void that spans through a level is drawn and selectable on every level it passes.
- **Connectors on the plan:** every stair, ramp, ladder, elevator and escalator that starts, ends, stops or passes on the active level is drawn with the geometry `packages/core/src/geometry/connectors.ts` derives (`packages/viewport/src/plan2d/connectors.ts`). A stair on the level it starts from shows its treads solid up to the plan cut (`rules.stairCutHeight` above the floor), a zigzag break line, then dashed treads, and an arrow with "UP" and its riser count; on the level it arrives at the whole stair is drawn with "DN"; on a level it only passes it is faint, with no arrow. A ramp shows its arrow and slope ("8.3 %"), a ladder its rails and rungs, an escalator its steps and arrow, an elevator its shaft, the car with its cross and a thick door mark on the side the door opens at that stop. A connector that breaks a limit is drawn in the warning colour. Connectors are on no layer, so they are always drawn and selectable: a click (ranked after columns, before walls) or a marquee selects one like an entity, the Properties panel shows its kind's fields (shape, risers, handrails, width, from and to level with offsets, a ramp's slope limit, a ladder's angle, an elevator's shaft, car, rotation and stops with door sides) and its derived numbers (risers, riser height, tread, flights, slope, run, travel) and broken limits read-only; a drag moves it, the grips of a stair or ramp reshape its walk line, R rotates, Shift+M mirrors (handrails and elevator doors swap sides), Delete removes it, Ctrl+C/V and Ctrl+A take it along. Every edit is one undo step; edits from the panel carry the change number, so an edit from a stale copy is refused.
- **Warnings:** the Warnings panel (`packages/modules/rules/`) lists what the design does against its rules; clicking a warning asks the plan to show its level and select its target (`PlanSelection.request`, applied once the level is active, with the view panned or zoomed out until it is in view).
- **Snapshots (`view.snapshot`):** main has no canvas, so the command emits `view.snapshot.request` and `SnapshotResponder` (`ui/snapshot-responder.ts`, started by the module's `setup`) draws the level in the window with `renderLevelToPng` (light colours, no grid, the mirror's document, loaded if the project has no tab) and answers through the hidden `view.snapshot.reply`; the command returns `{ mimeType: 'image/png', data, levelId, levelName, width, height }` (`output: 'image'`, so MCP sends image content and the assistant an image block). Without a window it fails with `UNAVAILABLE` after 10 s, on a level with nothing drawn with `NOT_FOUND`.
- **Thumbnails:** the plan view draws the ground floor into an offscreen canvas (480 × 360, light colours, no grid) 1.5 s after a project's last change, and at once when its tab is left or closed, and stores it through the hidden `project.thumbnail.set`; the Home card shows it. An empty ground floor leaves the card's placeholder.
- **Performance:** everything derived is cached by object identity in the renderer and the scene is an R-tree culled to the view, so panning and zooming never rebuild geometry. Measured, not promised: `packages/viewport/src/plan2d/perf.test.ts` derives 5,000 walls and indexes them under a loose bound, and `src/main/smoke/perf.ts` adds 5,000 walls in three batches, pans and zooms with real input and reads the frame time from the canvas (mean under 100 ms; the numbers are logged).
- **`plan.apply`** runs a list of editing commands as one transaction: all succeed or nothing changes, and it is one undo step. A step can name what it creates (`as: "w1"`) and later steps refer to it as `"$w1"`. `dryRun` checks without applying. Only editing commands can be steps; batches cannot be nested. A batch of 20 steps or more by an agent first keeps the project in an automatic snapshot.

## Storage

Entities live in their level's file (see the library README). The plan's camera, the reference underlay choice and the chosen main view are view state, stored per machine in the project's `.local/ui.json` (`camera`, `underlay`, `centerView`) next to the active level. Snapping, grid and tool defaults are global settings: the `editor` section of `config.json` (see `packages/core/README.md`), written through the hidden `config.update` like every setting.

## Agents

All allowed and undoable: `view.snapshot` (one level's plan as a PNG image, the ground level by default, at most 2048 px a side), `plan.describe` (the whole design as a reader would describe it, derived on the spot: every level with its entity counts, its rooms with net areas and perimeters, each room's doors and windows with the room they lead to or `outside`, which rooms are adjacent and whether through a wall, a door or a window, the connectors landing in each room and the enclosed spaces no room names yet; then the vertical circulation with the room each stair, ramp, ladder, escalator or elevator opens into on every level, the slabs it cuts and its derived numbers; `packages/core/src/describe/describe.ts`, which probes both sides of every wall and opening and the foot and head of every connector against the room polygons), `plan.query` (entities by level, layer, type or id), `plan.layer.add/update/move/remove`, `plan.wall.add` (one straight or arc wall from two points, a run of points as one wall per segment or a single wall, or a full path; thickness, height and alignment), `plan.opening.add` (a door, window or plain opening in a wall at `along` or at the point nearest to `at`, with sizes and swing; refused with the free ranges when it does not fit), `plan.room.add` (a room from a seed point inside walls, or from a region), `plan.room.detect` (a room for every enclosed space without one, skipping slivers, shafts and voids), `plan.rooms` (every room with whether it is enclosed or shares its space, its net area in mm², perimeter, label point and outline), `plan.slab` (a level's slabs as derived, with the connector openings cut out: polygons, area in mm², and each opening with its connector), `plan.shape.add` (a free shape from points, with arcs by bulge and closed by default, or from a full path with corners; `filled`, `name`, any layer), `plan.dimension.add` (aligned, horizontal, vertical, angular or radius; `anchors` tie an end to a vertex of a wall's line or face or of a shape on the same level), `plan.dimensions` (every dimension with its points as resolved, what it reads in mm or degrees and the text shown), `plan.transform` (move by a vector, rotate about a pivot, mirror across a line, optionally as copies; connector ids are accepted next to entity ids; a moved opening slides along its wall, a mirror swaps a door's swing side and a stair's handrail or an elevator's door sides; `nudge` merges quick repeats into one undo step), `plan.path.vertex.move/insert/remove` (also the walk line of a stair or ramp, by its connector id), `plan.path.segment.set` (straight, or an arc by bulge or through a point) and `plan.path.corner.set` (fillet, chamfer or sharp) on any entity's path (`which`: a wall's, shape's or divider's path or a region's outline, or a region's hole), `plan.entity.add/update/delete`, `plan.apply`. The path commands and `plan.entity.update` take `baseSeq` and `epoch` for the conflict check above. Agents send intent (add this wall, set this field); the change is built from the current document in the main process, never from a possibly stale copy.
