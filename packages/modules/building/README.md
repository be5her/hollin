# Building

The building of a project: its levels (floors, basements, mezzanines) and the vertical connectors between them (stairs, ladders, ramps, elevators, escalators).

- **Levels** have a name, a floor-to-floor height and a slab thickness. **Elevations are derived, never stored:** the ground level sits at the building's datum (0 by default); levels stacked above it add up the heights below them; basements hang below it, each its own height lower (negative elevations); mezzanines and split levels sit at an offset from a stacked host level. So changing a level's height moves every level above it, a deeper basement pushes the ones below it down, and grade stays put. Setting a level's elevation changes the value that produces it: the datum for the ground floor (the whole building moves), the height of the level below for a level above ground, its own height for a basement, its offset for a mezzanine.
- Levels are added on top, at the bottom, or above/below another level; duplicated (the copy, with new ids and its references remapped, is stacked right above); reordered by dragging; removed (levels above drop by its height; stairs and ramps ending on it go; elevators skip it; columns and shafts that reached up to it stop at their own level). A level that hosts a mezzanine cannot be removed until the mezzanine is moved or removed. A mezzanine can be moved to another stacked level (`hostLevelId`): it keeps its elevation when the new host reaches it, otherwise it sits on the new host's floor. `levelRemovalImpact` (core) says what a removal would take with it, grouped, without changing anything.
- **Connectors** belong to the building, not to a level: a stair (straight, L, U, winder, spiral or curved, along an axis path, with riser and tread intent), ladder, ramp (with a slope limit), elevator (shaft and car size, the levels it stops at and the door side at each), escalator. A connector's top must be above its bottom when it is added or changed. Its geometry is derived from the levels' elevations (`packages/core/src/geometry/connectors.ts`), so a level's height change regenerates every stair on it: risers, riser height, tread depth, flights and landings; a ramp's slope; the slabs it cuts. The limits it is checked against are global settings (**Settings > Rules**, the `rules` section of `config.json`); a stair's own riser count, riser limit and tread limit take precedence.
- **The project tab** shows the levels from top to bottom with their elevations in display units, the active level's name, elevation, height and slab (typed in any unit: `3.6m`, `3600`, `11' 10"`), its layers (show/hide, lock, opacity), and a section through the whole building with every level, the ground line and the connectors (a stair drawn with its derived risers). Click a level, in the list or the section, to make it active. A level's name is edited in place (double-click it, or F2 on the focused row; Enter keeps, Escape drops). Its right-click menu adds a level above or below (asking for name, height and slab), a mezzanine (name, offset above the floor, height, slab), moves a mezzanine to another level (also the **Placed from** select in the details), and removes the level after listing what goes with it: everything on it by type, the stairs, ramps, ladders and escalators that arrive there, the elevators that lose the stop, and spans cut short; a level that hosts a mezzanine says which to move first.

- **The 3D view** (the **3D** tab next to Plan and Section, `ui/scene-view.ts`) shows the whole building: every level at its elevation with its walls (openings cut through, glass in the windows), slabs (stairwells, shafts and voids cut out), columns, and the stairs, ramps, ladders, escalators and elevators between them, built from the solids core derives and drawn by `@studio/viewport`'s `scene3d`. A change made anywhere (the plan, the panels, an agent) shows at once; only the levels it touched are rebuilt. Hidden layers are left out, as on the plan. Drag to orbit, right-drag or Shift+drag to pan, the wheel zooms at the cursor, Home or the fit button shows everything; the toolbar's 3/4, Top, Front and Side look from the south-west above, from straight above with north up, from the south and from the east. It follows the light or dark theme (`--scene-*` in `styles.css`). Without WebGL it says so and the other views keep working.
  - **All**, **Isolate** and **Cutaway** choose the levels shown: every level; the active level alone, with the connectors that start on it; or the active level and every level below it, with its walls, columns and windows cut at the plan cut height (the `rules.stairCutHeight` setting, 1200 mm by default) and caps on the cut, and the stairs, rails, elevator cars and shafts clipped by a plane there. **Explode** spreads the levels apart, up to 4 m between one level and the next; a connector moves with the level it starts from.
  - The active level is the one the plan, the levels panel and the section use: the toolbar's level select, a level clicked in the list or the section, or a click on anything drawn in 3D (without dragging) changes it everywhere.
  - The switch at the right of the Plan | Section | 3D bar shows the plan **side by side** with the chosen view, so walls drawn on the plan can be watched going up in 3D.
  - **Decor:** furniture and lights are drawn in 3D (see `packages/modules/decor/README.md`); **Day | Night** in the toolbar switches between the sky and sun and the design's own lights (the 16 nearest the camera), kept in the view state; a click on a piece of furniture selects it on the plan. Doors and windows show their frames, leaves (open at 90° on their swing side) and casements from their style.
  - **Walking:** a double click on a floor (a tread, a landing, an elevator car) starts the walk camera there, eyes 1.6 m up, facing away from the orbit camera (on a sofa or against a wall, at the nearest clear spot beside it). W, A, S, D or the arrow keys walk (Shift runs), up stairs and ramps and down stairwells, through doors but not through walls or windows (a walker closer than its radius to one can still step away or along it, never closer); a drag looks around; PageUp and PageDown ride the elevator you stand in; Escape goes back to the orbit camera where it was. Every level shows, whole and in place, while walking; the footer names the level you are on. The walking rules are core's (`packages/core/src/solids/walk.ts`).

## Storage

Levels, layers and connectors live in `project.json`; each level's entities in its own level file (see the library README). The active level, the open right panel and the 3D view's camera and display (`view3d`: `orbit` with target, distance, azimuth and elevation; `mode`; `explode`; `lighting`, day or night) are view state, stored per machine in `.local/ui.json`.

## Agents

All allowed; every change is an undoable transaction attributed to the agent.

- `building.get`: levels bottom to top with derived elevation, height, top, slab, whether it is the ground or a basement, its layers and entity counts; the connectors, each with `derived`: bottom, top and rise, the levels whose slab it cuts, the limits it breaks, and per kind its risers, riser height, tread depth and flights, slope, run or stops.
- `building.level.add` (with `position` or `mezzanineOf`), `building.level.update` (name, height, slab, elevation, ground, `hostLevelId` for a mezzanine), `building.level.duplicate`, `building.level.move`, `building.level.remove`, and the read-only `building.level.impact` (what a removal would do).
- `building.connector.add` takes a full record or an intent form: a stair by `shape`, `from`, `to`, `bottomLevelId` and `topLevelId` (plus `width`, `turn`), whose walk line is laid out from the rise and the rule limits (an L turns at the right angle on the turn's side, a U runs two flights a width apart as long as their treads need, a curve is a quarter circle, a spiral sweeps around `from` as far as the treads need), a ramp by `from`, `to` and its levels, an elevator by `at`, `fromLevelId` and `toLevelId` (it stops at every stacked level between, with `door` on each). Refused with INVALID_INPUT when the points allow no such stair or the elevator has fewer than two stops.
- `building.connector.update` (with optional `baseSeq` and `epoch`: an update from a stale copy is refused with CONFLICT, naming who changed it meanwhile, as `plan.entity.update` does), `building.connector.remove`.
- All of these can be steps of `plan.apply`.
