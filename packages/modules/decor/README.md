# Decor

Furniture, lights and finishes: the 3D layer of a design.

- **The built-in catalog** ships with the app as code, not files (`packages/core/src/decor/builtin.ts`): 31 parametric furniture recipes in eight categories (living: sofas with 2 and 3 seats, an armchair, a coffee table, a TV unit, a bookshelf; dining: tables for 4 and 6, a chair; bedroom: single and double beds, a nightstand, a wardrobe, a dresser; kitchen: a base cabinet, a wall cabinet, a 2.4 m counter, a fridge, a cooker, a sink unit; bathroom: a toilet, a washbasin, a bathtub, a shower; office: a desk, an office chair; lighting: a pendant, a floor lamp and a table lamp, each with a bulb point and its lumens; outdoor: a potted plant, a tree), 38 materials (paints, timber and floorboards, tiles, stone, concrete, carpet, brick, fabrics, metals, glass, ceramic, plants) with PBR parameters and an optional procedural pattern (planks, tiles, brick, herringbone or noise, with its repeat in mm and a second colour), and 7 door and window styles (flush, panelled and glazed doors; fixed, single and double casement and mullioned windows). A recipe is boxes (with rounded vertical edges) and cylinders or cones, each in a named material slot with a default material; `recipes.ts` turns it into one mesh per slot (the solids' pipeline: no model file needed) and into its plan outline (the parts seen from above, united with Clipper). Its bottom centre is the origin and its front faces −y, like an imported model. They are referenced as `builtin:<id>`; the ids are stable, since projects store them.
- **Searching** (`catalog.search`) covers built-ins, imported models and models only the project has, in one result shape (`CatalogEntry` in `packages/core/src/decor/catalog.ts`), ranked with the palette's fuzzy score over the name, then an exact tag or category, then the tags and category fuzzily.
- **The catalog** holds the furniture models a project can use. Models imported by the user live in the library (`catalog/furniture/`) and are shared by every project; a project keeps its own copy of each model it uses, so it never needs the catalog again (packages, snapshots and other machines carry the model with the project).
- **Importing a model** (`catalog.import`): a `.glb` file, or a `.gltf` whose buffers are embedded as data URIs, up to 50 MB. The file is checked before anything is stored: the GLB header and chunks, the JSON, every buffer view inside its buffer and every accessor inside its buffer view, the meshes, nodes and scenes it names (and no cycles), and no outside files. Draco and meshopt compression and KTX2 textures are refused with a clear message (the app ships no decoders for them). The model is measured from its position accessors' bounds through the node transforms, and glTF's metres with y up become the app's millimetres with z up (a model's front, +z in glTF, faces −y on the plan). It is stored as `<sha256>.glb` (a `.gltf` is packed into a GLB first), named from its file name, in the category `imported`, with the file name's words as tags. Importing the same file again lists it once. GLB reading and writing is `packages/core/src/decor/glb.ts`, no library: the writer makes the smoke test's model and will make Milestone 9's export.
- **References** follow one grammar (`packages/core/src/decor/refs.ts`): `asset:<sha256>.<ext>` (a model in the catalog and in a project's assets), `builtin:<id>` (furniture, materials and styles that ship with the app) and `paint:#rrggbb` (any colour, as a material). Items take models, finishes and slots take materials, openings take styles; the schema checks the form, and the transaction checks that every `asset:` an item uses is in the project's registry.
- **The Catalog panel** (a tab in the project's right column, `ui/catalog-panel.ts`): Furniture and Materials, a search box, category chips and a grid of pictures. Built-in furniture is drawn in the window on demand as a small axonometric picture of its recipe (`drawThumbnail` in `@studio/viewport`: triangles sorted back to front on a 2D canvas, no WebGL), materials as a swatch of their pattern; both are kept in memory. An imported model is drawn once from its GLB (`readGlbTriangles` in core) and stored with the library through the hidden `catalog.thumbnail.set`, then shown over `library://`. A click on a model arms the plan's **Furniture tool** with it (`PlanTools` in the plan module); **Import…** picks a `.glb` or `.gltf` with the native dialog and runs `catalog.import`; a right click on an imported model removes it from the catalog after asking.
- **Items on the plan**: each item is drawn as its footprint (a recipe's parts seen from above united, or an imported model's extent), scaled, turned and placed, filled lightly in `--plan-decor` and outlined, with the parts seen from above as thin inner lines (cushions, a back, pillows), a short line inside its front edge and its name when it is large enough on screen, at its decor layer's opacity. It is hit by its footprint, taken by marquees, moved like any entity (Alt-drag, arrows, `plan.transform`; a mirror flips the model along its own x, as a negative x scale), and when selected it shows one round grip in front of it: drag it round to turn the item to face the cursor (held on 15° steps within 3°, Alt free, or type an angle). The **Furniture tool** (`I`, or the toolbar's sofa) places the armed model: a dashed ghost follows the snapped cursor, `R` turns it 15° (`Shift+R` back, or Shift with the wheel), a click places it and the tool stays for the next, Escape ends it. The Properties panel shows an item's model, its position, height above the floor and turn, its size typed in display units (stored as the scale of the model's own size), and a material for each slot (a built-in, or any colour).
- **Lights** are entities on the decor layer: a point light (a bulb), a spot (aimed at a target, or straight down, with a cone) or an area light (a 600 mm panel shining down), with a colour and an intensity in lumens. `decor.light.add` adds one; the plan's **Light tool** (`G`) places one at a click with the kind, height and lumens from its options bar; the plan draws a light as a small sun (a square for a panel, a line to a spot's target); the Properties panel edits its kind, position, height, lumens, colour, and a spot's cone and target. A lamp from the catalog (pendant, floor and table lamps) carries a bulb point and its lumens: placed **with its light** (the Furniture tool's option, or `withLight`) it gets a warm point light there, named after it.
- **In 3D** (`@studio/viewport`'s `scene3d/decor.ts`): each level holds its furniture and lights, kept in step item by item by identity. A built-in is its recipe's slot meshes (shared geometry) in its slots' materials; an imported model is loaded once per file with three's `GLTFLoader` from `library://project/<id>/assets/<file>`, turned from glTF's metres with y up into millimetres with z up, cloned per item, its material slots repainted by name; a grey box of its extent stands in while it loads or if it cannot. Items are placed by `itemMatrix` (core), drawn in physically based materials (`scene3d/materials.ts`, one per material reference), cut by the cutaway's plane like the rest, and picked: a click on one in the 3D view selects it on the plan. The view's **Day | Night** switch (kept in the project's `view3d.lighting`): Day is the sky and the key light; Night a dim sky, a dark background and the design's lights, the 16 nearest the camera switched on (no shadows, so software GL keeps up), with three's `PointLight`, `SpotLight` or `RectAreaLight` (`RectAreaLightUniformsLib`, part of three) and the lumens turned into candela (a point: lm / 4π) times a fixed scale. The walk camera walks round furniture: an item stops the walker at the knee, a pendant overhead does not.
- **Finishes** (`decor.material.set`): a material for one face of a wall (`wall.materials.left` or `right`, looking along its path), or for a room's floor or ceiling (`room.finish`); a room's **walls** finish writes the material onto every wall face that looks into the room (probed along both sides of every wall), so each face has one source and one undo step covers them all; `null` clears. Materials are built-ins or any `paint:#rrggbb`. They need no change to the wall solids: core's `solids/finishes.ts` builds thin **skins**: a strip 2 mm out of each painted face, carried past the wall's open ends (so an outer corner is covered), cut by the walls of each height band (so it stops where another wall abuts the face) and kept within 3 mm of them (so it ends with the wall and leaves its doors and windows open), built band by band with caps only where the strip changes (no seam at a door's head); a floor cover 2 mm thick over the room's net area and a ceiling cover under the ceiling. Every mesh carries texture coordinates in millimetres (a face turned up or down by its plan position, any other by its distance along the face and its height), and a material's pattern is drawn as a repeating `CanvasTexture` of its real size (`scene3d/materials.ts`), so floorboards, tiles and bricks keep their scale on any face. The walls, slabs and the rest switched to physically based materials too, so the design's lights light everything alike.
- **Painting** (`ui/catalog-panel.ts`, `PlanTools` in the plan module): a click on a material in the Catalog panel arms it. The plan's **Paint tool** then lights up the wall face under the cursor (the side of the wall it is on) or the room's floor, and a click gives it the material; in the 3D view a click on a wall face, a floor or a ceiling gives it the material (`surfaceAt` in core: the wall face from the hit point and its normal, the room under a floor or ceiling); Escape (or another tool) ends painting. The Properties panel shows and changes a wall's two faces and a room's floor, walls (one material, or mixed) and ceiling.
- **Doors and windows** get their 3D parts from their style (`style`, a `builtin:` door or window style; a door without one is a flush door in a frame, a window a fixed window in a frame; core's `solids/openings.ts`): a frame lining the opening (jambs and head, and under a window a sill board) standing out of the wall faces as the style says; a door's leaf (flush, panelled or glazed) hung on the hinge jamb's inner edge, open at 90° on its swing side; a window's casements and mullions in the middle of the wall. They are drawn in the style's frame and leaf materials and cut by the cutaway like the walls.
- **`plan.describe`** lists each room's furniture (name, model, position, turn, size as placed, the area it covers), lights and finishes (floor, walls, ceiling), and furniture and lights in no room under `outside`.
- **Placing an item** (`decor.item.place`) puts a model's bottom centre at a point of a level, at a height above its floor (by default the model's usual height: a wall cabinet at 1450 mm, a pendant at 2200 mm, else the floor), turned on the plan, sized by `size` in mm or by `scale`, with slots repainted by `materials` (a built-in material or a `paint:` colour; an imported model's slots are its glTF material names). A library model is copied into the project's `assets/` and registered in the same undoable change.

## Storage

```
<library>/catalog/furniture/<sha256>.glb   imported models
<library>/catalog/furniture/<sha256>.png   their thumbnails (drawn by the window)
<library>/catalog/furniture/index.json     name, category, tags, size, low corner, bytes, triangles per model:
                                           a cache, rebuilt from the files when missing, broken or out of step
<library>/projects/<id>/assets/<sha256>.glb  the project's copy of each model it uses
project.json `assets`                      the project's registry: per file, its name, kind, category, size and low corner
```

Every write is atomic (`writeFileAtomic`) and queued per file. The registry arrived with format 2 of `project.json`; a format 1 project is migrated (an empty registry) after an automatic snapshot. Removing a model from the catalog never touches the projects that use it.

The window reads models and thumbnails read-only through `library://project/<id>/assets/<sha256>.<ext>` and `library://catalog/furniture/<sha256>.glb|.png` (`libraryUrlFile` in `packages/core/src/node/library-urls.ts`: every segment matched whole, so nothing else in the library is reachable).

## Agents

- Allowed: `decor.material.set` and `decor.light.add` (also steps of `plan.apply`), `catalog.search` (items, materials or styles: built-ins, the library's models and the project's own, ranked by name, tags and category, with their size, slots, colour and source), `catalog.get`, `catalog.import` (from a file path), `decor.item.place` (also a step of `plan.apply`). Items are edited and removed with `plan.entity.update`, `plan.transform` and `plan.entity.delete`.
- Gated: `catalog.remove` (deletes from the library catalog for good).
- Not exposed: `catalog.thumbnail.set`.
- The design guidelines (`studio://guidelines`, and the in-app assistant's system prompt) carry a furnishing section: furnish after the rooms are named, how `at` and `rotation` place a model, typical furniture sizes and clearances, lights per room in lumens, finishes. The MCP prompt **`furnish-room`** (`room`, `style`) asks an agent to furnish, light and finish a room in one `plan_apply`, check it with `plan_describe` and look at it with `view_snapshot` in plan and in 3D by day and at night.
