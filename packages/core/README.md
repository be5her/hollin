# @studio/core

The engine. Everything here is pure TypeScript with no Electron, Node I/O or UI framework, so the renderer imports it too; Node-only storage lives in `src/node` and is imported as `@studio/core/node`.

## Commands

- `defineCommand({ id, title, description, scope, input, mutating?, hidden?, handler })` is a typed identity helper: the handler infers its input type from the zod schema.
- `defineModule({ id, commands, onStart?, onStop?, onProjectOpen?, onProjectClose? })` groups a feature's commands.
- `CommandRegistry.execute(id, rawInput, ctx)` checks, in order: unknown id → `UNKNOWN_COMMAND`; a project-scope command without a project → `NO_PROJECT`; `safeParse` failure → `INVALID_INPUT` (each issue as "path: message", also in `details`); the mutating gate; then the handler.
- The mutating gate never applies to the UI. MCP callers are refused (`MUTATION_BLOCKED`) unless `config.mcp.allowMutations` is on. The assistant goes through `host.confirmMutation`, which asks the user (`MUTATION_DECLINED` when they say no; refused while no host provides it). A function-valued `mutating` decides per call, after validation.
- `registry.list()` gives `{ id, module, title, description, scope, mutating, conditional, hidden, noInput }` for the palette and MCP.
- `StudioError(code, message, details?)` is the one error type; `toErrorPayload` turns anything thrown into `{ code, message, details? }`.

## Storage (`src/node`)

- `writeFileAtomic(file, data)`: a uniquely named temp file next to the target, fsync, then rename. The rename is retried with backoff for about two seconds on `EPERM`/`EBUSY`/`EACCES`, which Windows reports while an antivirus scanner, the indexer or a preview pane holds the file. The temp file is removed on failure. `renameWithRetry` does the same for folders.
- `WriteQueue` runs writes one after another per key; a failed write rejects its caller but never blocks later writes.
- `ConfigStore`: `config.json` in the app's data folder. A file that is not JSON is kept aside as `config.corrupt-<time>.json` and the app starts from defaults. Every update is written atomically and announced (`config.changed`).
- `SecretStore`: named secrets encrypted with the OS keychain (Electron `safeStorage`) in `secrets.json`. Without OS encryption (or with Linux's fixed-key `basic_text` backend) nothing is written and secrets last for the session only; `persistent` says which.
- `project-store.ts`: reads and writes a project folder with the commit protocol (content-hashed level files, `project.json` last, then old files removed), so a crash never mixes old and new files. Only changed levels are stringified.
- `project-session.ts`: one open project's document and its autosave (750 ms debounce, 5 s maximum wait, clean only after a successful write, retries on failure, flush on close).
- `library.ts`: the library folder: index (a rebuildable cache), listing and search, create, duplicate, trash, snapshots, thumbnails, packages, the lock heartbeat.
- `package.ts`: export and import of `.psp` packages with the import guards.
- `catalog.ts`: the library's furniture catalog (`catalog/furniture/<sha256>.glb` with a rebuildable `index.json`): import with checks and hashing, remove, thumbnails, copying a model into a project's assets. See `packages/modules/decor/README.md`.
- `library-urls.ts`: `libraryUrlFile`, the only files `library://` serves (project thumbnails and assets, catalog models and thumbnails).
- `project-service.ts`: the library plus the open projects; what `host.projects` is. See `packages/modules/library/README.md`.

## Geometry (`src/geometry`)

`path.ts`: the `Path` primitive (vertices with line, arc-by-bulge or cubic segments and fillet/chamfer corners), `resolvePath` (corners applied exactly for line and arc neighbours, clamped with a diagnostic when they do not fit), length, signed area (exact for arcs and cubics), bounding box, flattening within a tolerance, point and tangent at a distance, nearest point, containment, reversal and transforms, plus the line–circle and circle–circle intersections. `vec.ts`: 2D vector helpers; points are readonly tuples in mm, y up.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `polygon.ts` | Flattened polygons: signed area, perimeter, box, containment, orientation, centroid, edge distance. |
| `clip.ts` | Clipper2 (`clipper2-ts`, a pure TypeScript port, pinned exactly to `2.0.1-18`) behind a millimetre interface: unions as a tree of outlines and holes, differences, intersections, offsets of polygons and polylines, morphological closing. Coordinates are scaled to integer micrometres. |
| `offset.ts` | `offsetPath`: a path moved to its left or right exactly (lines shift, arcs stay concentric, neighbours are trimmed or extended to meet, sharp corners bevel at the miter limit); null when it cannot be exact (Bézier segments, a vanishing arc, a flipped segment). `extendPathEnd` carries an open path on along its tangent. |
| `walls.ts` | A wall's body from its reference line, thickness and alignment (exact faces, or a flattened offset as the fallback), the ends of open walls clustered into junction nodes, the corner fills where two ends meet (the overlap of both walls carried on past the node: the quadrilateral bounded by the four face lines), and `joinWalls`, the union of bodies and fills. |
| `intersect.ts` | Segment–segment intersections (lines and arcs, within the segments or extended), the perpendicular foot and tangent points from a point, for snapping and joins. |
| `transform.ts` | `transformEntity`: every entity type moved, rotated about a pivot or mirrored across a line (openings stay on their wall; a mirror swaps a door's swing and flips arcs), and `changedFields` for the resulting update. |
| `path-edit.ts` | Vertex, segment and corner edits on a path, and `pathOfEntity`/`withPath` to address a wall's, shape's or divider's path or a region's outline or hole. |

| `corners.ts` | The corner grip's geometry: where a fillet's arc or a chamfer's line crosses the vertex's bisector (`cornerFrame`, `cornerGripPoint`), the largest fillet the edges allow, and the corner a grip dragged to a point means (`cornerFromGrip`: the radius, or both chamfer distances in their ratio; onto the vertex, sharp). Straight edges only; the inspector covers the rest. |
| `openings.ts` | `openingFrame` (an opening's centre, jambs and cut through its wall's body), `fitOpening` (whether an opening fits between the wall's ends and its other openings, the nearest place that does, the free ranges), `openingMisfits`, `columnPolygon` and `openingSymbol` (the jambs, a window's glazing lines, a door's leaf and swing arc: what the plan and the exports draw). |
| `rooms.ts` | Rooms: the solids (joined walls, columns, hair-thin dividers) closed by a millimetre and unioned into a tree; every hole at every depth is a cavity with its islands, net area and perimeter; a detected room resolves to the cavity its seed lies in (not enclosed when none, shared when another room claimed it first), a drawn room to its region; unclaimed cavities for detection (slivers, shafts and voids skipped); the automatic slab (outlines that enclose something). |
| `connectors.ts` | Vertical connectors derived from the levels' elevations, never stored: `deriveConnector` gives a stair its risers (`riser.count`, or the rise over the riser limit rounded up) and riser height, flights split at the axis's sharp corners by landings that take half the width either side (a U's short middle leg becomes one half-landing; a winder winds its treads round the inside corner instead), the treads shared between flights by length, tread lines perpendicular to the walk line (radial on arcs: curved and spiral stairs), handrails 50 mm inside, the footprint, and the riser where the plan cut falls; a ramp its slope over its sloped length; a ladder its run from its angle; an escalator its 30° run with landings; an elevator its shaft, car and a door mark per stop, bottom to top. | `describe/describe.ts` | `describeDesign`: the design as a reader would describe it, for agents. Per level the rooms (net area, perimeter, enclosed, shared), each room's openings with the room on the other side (or outside), adjacency between rooms (through a wall, a door, a window or an opening: both sides of every wall are probed every 600 mm and both sides of every opening), the connectors landing in each room and the enclosed spaces no room names; then the vertical circulation with the room each connector opens into on every level (just before a stair's first riser and past its last, in front of an elevator's door), the slabs it cuts and `connectorNumbers`, its derived numbers rounded for reading. | `rules/check.ts` | The design against its rules, pure: `checkConnectorRules` (every limit a connector breaks, from `deriveConnector`), `checkLevelRules` (doors and windows that do not fit, detected rooms not enclosed, paths that cross themselves, connector openings outside a floor's slab or on a floor without one) and `checkRules` (all of it). | `connector-intent.ts` | `stairAxisFrom`: a stair's walk line laid out from its start and end per shape (straight; L and winder with the corner at the right angle on the turn's side; U as two flights a width apart as long as their treads need; a quarter-circle curve; a spiral swept around its centre as far as the treads need, in arcs of at most 180°), `stairSteps` (the riser count from the limit and a comfortable tread), and `connectorFromIntent`: the full record a stair, ramp or elevator intent stands for (`building.connector.add` accepts both). |
| `connector-edit.ts` | Connectors under the plan's editing commands: `isConnectorId` (by the id prefix), `connectorPath`/`withConnectorPath` (the walk line of a stair or ramp as the path the `plan.path.*` commands edit) and `transformConnector` (move, rotate, mirror of an axis or of `at`/`direction`/`rotation`; a mirror swaps a stair's handrails and an elevator's front and back doors). |
| `plan-symbols.ts` | The pure geometry of the plan's symbols, sized by the caller (a few pixels on the canvas, a fraction of the text height in an export): `PLAN_GLYPHS` (how far point and light glyphs reach), `walkPoints`/`walkSegments`/`walkTangent` along a connector's walk line, `arrowHeadLines` and `breakLinePoints` (the zigzag where the plan cuts a stair). |
| `level-geometry.ts` | `deriveWalls` / `deriveRooms` / `deriveLevelGeometry`: everything a level's entities derive to (walls joined and cut, cavities, rooms, the slab), uncached. The renderer memoises by object identity (valid there, where the mirror builds a fresh draft per patch); main derives afresh, because a transaction mutates a level's record in place while a batch runs. |

## Solids (`src/solids`)

The building in 3D as pure data: triangle meshes in millimetres with z up, no three and no DOM, so they are tested in node and drawn by `@studio/viewport`'s `scene3d`.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `mesh.ts` | `Mesh` (flat triangles, counter-clockwise seen from outside) and `MeshBuilder`: vertical sides of polygons between two heights, horizontal caps triangulated by `triangulatePolygons` in `clip.ts` (Clipper2's constrained Delaunay triangulation, part of `clipper2-ts`; vertices within 0.01 mm of the line through their neighbours are dropped first, since a boolean can leave a zero-width spike that makes it fill the holes while reporting success, as a wall top cap once covered the rooms under it), prisms, boxes with rounded vertical edges, cylinders and cones; every triangle gets texture coordinates in mm (`triangleUvs`: by plan position when it faces up or down, else along the face and up). `meshVolume` (by the divergence theorem; the same about any origin only when the solid is closed, which the tests use) and `meshBox`. |
| `building.ts` | `deriveLevelSolids` / `deriveBuildingSolids`. Walls are extruded in height bands instead of with CSG: every height where a wall starts or stops or an opening's sill or head lies splits them, each band is the joined walls present over it (the plan's joins, `deriveWalls`) minus the cuts of the openings spanning it, and the caps between bands are their differences, so sills, lintels and wall tops are exact. A wall of its level's height stops under the slab of the stacked level above (`levelCeiling`); one with its own height and base offset stops where it says. Windows get a 12 mm pane of glass in the middle of the wall; doors and plain openings stay open. Columns run to the slab above or to the top of their span. Slabs lie below the floor by their thickness (the entity's or the level's), with the connector openings cut out, and voids (the floors they show on) and shafts (every floor above the one they start from). Entities on hidden layers are left out. Each level also returns its wall bands and its floor (the slabs' top faces) for walking, and a cut height lowers its walls and columns for the cutaway view. `deriveBuildingSolids` gives every level and every connector. |
| `connectors.ts` | `deriveConnectorSolids`: each connector from its derived geometry (`deriveConnector`), placed with the level it starts from (an elevator's lowest stop). A stair's steps (each tread a block reaching one riser and 200 mm below it, or down to the floor), its landings at their derived heights and handrails 900 mm above the line through the nosings; a ramp's flights sloped between level landings; a ladder's rails and rungs; an escalator's 1200 mm landings and its incline; an elevator's glass shaft from its lowest stop to the ceiling of its highest, and its car. Each also lists the surfaces you can stand on (`WalkSurface`: a polygon and its plane), and an elevator its car and stops, for the walk camera. |
| `finishes.ts` | Surface finishes as thin skins: `addWallSkins` (a strip 2 mm out of each painted face per height band, carried round outer corners, cut by the walls, kept within 3 mm of them, so doors and windows stay open; built with caps only where it changes), `addRoomCovers` (a room's floor and ceiling covers over its net area), `wallSidesFacing` (the wall faces that look into a room), `surfaceAt` (the wall face, floor or ceiling under a 3D click, from the point and the face's normal), `sideOfWall`. |
| `openings.ts` | `openingParts`: a door's or window's 3D parts from its style (`openingStyleOf`): the frame lining the opening, a door's leaf open at 90° on its swing side (flush, panelled, or glazed with its pane), a window's casements and mullions, cut at the cutaway's height. `deriveLevelSolids` adds them as `frame` and `leaf` parts in the style's materials (`SolidPart.material`), and each item's footprint and heights as an obstacle for walking. |
| `walk.ts` | The walk camera's world, pure: `walkWorld` gathers the slab tops, the connectors' treads, landings and slopes, the elevator cars and the walls band by band. `floorAt` is the floor under a point for feet at a height: the highest surface no more than a step (`STEP_UP`, 450 mm) above them, so a stair is climbed and a stairwell dropped into, else the ground outside. `blocked` probes the walls at the knee (400 mm, where a window's sill stops you) and the head (1500 mm, under a door's head) within the walker's radius (250 mm), and furniture at the knee only. `walkBy`/`walkRelative` move in substeps of at most 100 mm, sliding along a wall met at an angle, the feet settling on the floor after each; `rideElevator` carries a walker standing in a car to the next stop up or down; `levelAt` names the level the feet are on. |

## Exchange (`src/exchange`)

Exports and imports as pure functions over the document; the `exchange` module (milestone 9) writes the files.

## Tests

`npm test` runs `*.test.ts` here: the registry and its gates, config merging and normalising, units, ids, atomic writes and the retry, the write queue, the config and secret stores, project folders and crash simulation, autosave timing, the library, packages, the project service, path geometry against analytic values, connector geometry against hand-computed riser counts, treads, landings, slopes and runs, elevations, transactions, history and references. `solids/solids.test.ts` checks the 3D solids against hand-computed volumes, each solid closed and facing outwards: a room's walls with a door and a window cut through, the sill, lintel and top caps at their heights, the window's glass, the slab under the floor, an L junction and an arc wall, walls stopping under the floor above or at their own height, a stairwell and a void cut out of the slabs, columns to the floor above or the top of their span, hidden layers left out, the cutaway cut, and 5,000 walls within a loose bound; `solids/connectors.test.ts` a straight stair's steps at their riser heights with the volume of its blocks and its handrails above the nosings, an L stair's landing, a ramp's even slope, an elevator's shaft and car, a ladder and an escalator; `solids/walk.test.ts` the floor under a point (slab, tread, ground outside, the stair under a stairwell), climbing a stair to the upper floor and stopping a radius short of the wall beyond, going out through a door but not through a wall or a window, sliding along a wall, and riding an elevator. `exchange/drawing.test.ts` draws a two-storey house (rooms, a door, a window, a stair, an elevator, a dimension, a column, furniture) and checks its layers and primitive counts, the walls' outline area against the hand-computed one, the door's swing arc as wide as the door, each room's name and area, the dimension's reading, the stair's treads, cut and UP/DN, spanning and hidden entities and the units; `exchange/dxf.test.ts` writes it as DXF and checks the sections and tables in order, one layer per drawing layer, every stroke and text read back (polylines within 0.001 mm, arcs with their centre and radius), the bulge sign of counter-clockwise and clockwise arcs, full turns, escapes and a CRLF file; `exchange/svg.test.ts` writes it as SVG and checks a well-formed, self-contained document, the sheet from the bounds or the paper, one named group per layer, the arc flags, y flipped with upright texts and escaping; `exchange/pdf.test.ts` writes both floors as PDF and checks the header and `%%EOF`, the xref offsets against their objects, the page count and content stream lengths, the texts, `fit` on A3, the deflate hook, WinAnsi with `m²` and the Béziers. `exchange/gltf.test.ts` writes the house, a face painted and an imported table upstairs, as GLB and checks it passes `inspectGlb`, the node names (project, levels, kinds, stair, elevator, furniture), the triangle count against the solids plus the furniture, the painted colour and the glass's blend read back, the ground floor's wall volume from the read-back triangles within 1 % of the solids', the table's placement, `includeItems` and `includeFinishes` off, and a missing model in `skipped`; `decor/glb.test.ts` also writes a node tree (names in order, one mesh with a primitive per part, shared materials, aligned views, min and max, a node matrix).
