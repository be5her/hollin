# @studio/viewport

Framework-free renderers driven by the document. `plan2d` draws and edits one level from above on a Canvas 2D; `scene3d` draws the whole building in 3D with three. The package is imported by the renderer only; main never loads it.

## plan2d

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `camera.ts` | The camera (`{ x, y, scale }`: the world point at the canvas centre in mm and the zoom in CSS pixels per mm), world ↔ screen mapping (the world is y-up, the screen y-down), zoom about a point, pan, fit to a box, the `1:N` drawing scale. |
| `theme.ts` | The colours the plan is drawn with, read from the CSS variables in `styles.css` (`--plan-*`, `--canvas`, `--fg` …) so the canvas follows the theme. `LIGHT_THEME` and `DARK_THEME` are fixed palettes for renders made without the window's theme (thumbnails, `view.snapshot`, `export.image`); `DARK_THEME` is the `.dark` block of `styles.css`, which `packages/modules/plan/snapshot-theme.test.ts` checks. |
| `scene.ts` | A level's entities in an R-tree (`rbush`), with a generous world box per entity type (a furniture item's from its footprint, worked out with the project's asset registry, which the scene keeps; items are measured again when the registry changes). `syncLevelScene` diffs by object identity: the mirror keeps untouched entities' objects through patches, so only changed entities are rebuilt. An opening follows its wall. |
| `draw.ts` | Tracing paths (lines, arcs by bulge, cubics) onto a `Path2D` or a context. |
| `render.ts` | One frame: background, the adaptive grid (minor and major lines by zoom, metric or imperial), the reference level faintly, then the entities in a fixed order (slab, rooms, voids, shapes, walls, columns, openings, fixtures, dimensions, labels), culled by the visible box, then text in screen space. Dimensions are resolved first (tied ends follow their wall or shape) and drawn from `dimensionGeometry` with extension lines, ticks, arcs and arrows; the Dimension tool previews with the same code. `Path2D` outlines are cached per entity and refilled, so panning and zooming never rebuild geometry. |
| `geometry-cache.ts` | Wall bodies, a level's joined walls (with exclusions for drag previews) and the whole level geometry (rooms, cavities, the slab) memoised by object identity (valid in the renderer only; see CLAUDE.md). The connector openings of a level (`cuts`) are memoised on the identity of the connectors, the building, the levels and the rule limits, and the slabs with them cut out on the identity of the uncut geometry and the cuts, so a stairwell costs nothing while nothing changes. The plan view and the Properties panel share one cache. |
| `connectors.ts` | The connectors a level shows (`planConnectors`: each with its role on the level, its derived geometry and a box; memoised by `LevelGeometryCache.connectorsOn`), the distance from a point to one (`connectorDistance`), and `drawConnector`: the symbol per kind and role (a stair's treads solid up to the plan cut, a break line and dashed treads beyond on its bottom level, whole with "DN" on its top level, faint where it only passes; the walk-line arrow with "UP"/"DN" and the riser count; a ramp's arrow and slope; a ladder's rails and rungs; an escalator's steps; an elevator's shaft, car cross and door mark per stop), in `--plan-connector` or the warning colour when a limit is broken. The symbols' geometry (walk line, arrow heads, break line; door and window symbols, dimension ticks) comes from core (`plan-symbols.ts`, `openingSymbol`, `dimensionMarks`), so the plan exports draw the same marks. |
| `hit.ts` | What is under a point: candidates from the R-tree, then an exact test per type (wall bodies, opening cuts, column footprints, regions, filled shapes anywhere inside and outlines or open lines along them, glyphs, dimension lines, label boxes), ranked smallest first (a door before its wall before a shape before the room). Hidden and locked layers are skipped. Connectors are hit by their footprint and rank after columns, before walls. Window and crossing marquees by entity and connector boxes. |
| `grips.ts` | The grips of a selected entity (its path's vertices and segment middles, holes included, and a corner grip on each fillet or chamfer between straight edges, placed by `cornerFrame`/`cornerGripPoint` from core; a dimension's points and its line; the walk line of a stair or ramp, `gripsOfConnector`), which one is under a screen point, and the `VertexRef` the inspector edits. |
| `snap.ts` | Snapping: a `SnapIndex` (an R-tree of everything on the level a point can snap to: wall reference lines and faces, region and shape outlines, dividers, opening jambs, centres; an endpoint snap names the wall or shape vertex it is, so a dimension can tie to it) and `snapPoint`, which ranks candidates within a tolerance by kind (endpoint, intersection, midpoint, centre, perpendicular or tangent from a start point, on a line, extension, angle step) then distance, with the grid as the fallback and Shift as a 45° constraint. `trackDirection` is the angle tracking: the ray from the start nearest the cursor among the main angles, the step's multiples and the parallels and perpendiculars of the `relativeTo` references, held while the cursor is within `TRACK_DEGREES` (3°, a quarter of the step when finer) or the pixel tolerance of it, so it is as easy to catch far from the start as near it; it ranks below the points aimed at and above a line under the cursor, and the ray's crossings with nearby segments count as intersections. Snaps on a segment carry its `tangent` so a tool can keep the wall its run started on as a reference (`referenceFrom`). Pure and unit-tested. |
| `tools/` | The tools as state machines fed pointer and key events, drawing their previews and committing through commands: `select.ts` (click, Shift, marquees, drag-move with the walls joined live, connectors selected, moved, reshaped by their axis grips, deleted, copied and pasted like entities (pasted on the level they came from, entities keep their layers), Ctrl-copy, nudges, Delete, Ctrl+A/C/V/D, rotate about a pivot, mirror across two points; grip drags that move a vertex with the wall ends that meet it, curve a segment through the cursor or split it, select a corner, F/C corners, size a corner by its grip or a typed length) `wall.ts` (runs of walls, arcs, closing) `opening.ts` (doors and windows placed where the cursor projects on a hovered wall, moved into the nearest free gap, a typed distance from the wall start, flips while placing), `room.ts` (the enclosed space under the cursor lit up with its area, a click makes it a room), `separator.ts` (room divider lines) and `shape.ts` (one class, three tools: a polyline with arcs, closed on its first point or with `C`, a rectangle from two corners, a circle from its centre and a point; what the outline makes is an option: a free shape, or a room, slab or void region, always closed and counter-clockwise), `dimension.ts` (aligned, horizontal, vertical, angular and radius dimensions from clicked or typed points and a clicked or typed distance, tied to the wall or shape corners the points snapped to), `label.ts` (a click asks the host for text and places the label), `connectors.ts` (the Stair, Ramp, Ladder and Elevator tools: walk lines clicked with tracking and typed lengths, a U or spiral laid out by `stairAxisFrom`, the levels from the options or the active level and the one above, previews through `previewConnector`) and `column.ts` (a column placed at a snapped click with its size, rotation and span). `tool.ts` is the contract: a `ToolHost` gives a tool commands, the entities, the layers, the levels and the rule limits, the mirror's change number for stale checks, the selection and the selected vertex, hit-testing and the joined walls. |
| `tools/paint.ts` | The Paint tool: the wall face (the side of the wall under the cursor) or the room's floor (anywhere in its space) lit up, a click giving it the armed material through `decor.material.set`. |
| `tools/light.ts` | The Light tool: a light of the options' kind, height and lumens at the snapped click, through `decor.light.add`. |
| `tools/item.ts` | The Furniture tool: the armed model's ghost at the snapped cursor, `R`/`Shift+R`/Shift+wheel turning 15°, a click placing it through `decor.item.place`. |
| `offscreen.ts` | `renderLevelToPng`: one level fitted into a canvas the caller makes, drawn with the same renderer and returned as PNG bytes (base64): the project thumbnail, `view.snapshot` and `export.image`. `theme` picks the palette; `transparent` clears the canvas instead of painting the paper (the frame's `transparent` flag in `render.ts`). The columns, shafts and voids spanning up from other levels are drawn as on the plan view (`entitiesOnLevel`). |
| `editor.ts` | `Plan2dEditor`: attach to a canvas, feed it the document, the active level, the underlay mode, the theme, the units and the tool options; it keeps a scene for the active level and the reference level only (each level's entities plus the columns, shafts and voids of other levels that span through it, `entitiesOnLevel`, merged into a record kept while nothing changes), owns the selection (pruned on every document change) and the clipboard, renders at most once per animation frame (`invalidate()`), and handles the mouse and keyboard: wheel zooms at the cursor, middle-drag or Space+drag pans, Home fits, PageUp/PageDown ask the host to change level, V, W, D, N, R, S, P, K, O, L, T, E and C pick tools, Shift+S, Shift+R and Shift+L the stair, ramp and ladder, Escape cancels. A digit typed while a tool waits for a point asks the host to open an input at the cursor; `typed(text)` parses the coordinate grammar and hands the point to the tool; a tool may ask the host for a line of text the same way (`requestText`). `revealSelection()` pans (or zooms out, never in) until the selection is in view. Callbacks report the camera, the cursor, the snap, the tool and its hint, the selection and the selected vertex, a right click, and frame statistics. |

The Angular host (`packages/modules/plan/ui/plan-view.ts`) owns the canvas element, sizes it with a `ResizeObserver` times `devicePixelRatio`, re-reads the theme when `<html class>` changes, persists the camera and mirrors the editor's state into `data-camera="x,y,scale"`, `data-level`, `data-underlay`, `data-entities`, `data-frame-level` (the level the last frame drew) and `data-frame-ms` attributes on the canvas, which the smoke test reads to convert millimetres to window pixels and to assert without any globals.

## decor

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `thumbnail.ts` | `drawThumbnail`: a model's triangles drawn from the south-west and above onto a 2D canvas, sorted back to front and shaded (no WebGL, so the Catalog panel can draw dozens); `recipeThumbParts` (a recipe's slot meshes in their materials), `materialColor`, `linearToHex`. |
| `patterns.ts` | `drawMaterialTile`: one seamless tile of a material's procedural pattern (planks, tiles, brick, a chevron parquet, mottled noise) with `patternTile` its size in mm; shared by the catalog's swatches and the 3D view's textures. |

## scene3d

The whole building in 3D, drawn with three from the solids `@studio/core` derives (`deriveLevelSolids`, `deriveConnectorsSolids`; see `packages/core/README.md`).

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `orbit.ts` | The orbit camera in the building's terms: the point looked at (mm, z up), the distance, and the direction looked from (azimuth counter-clockwise from +x on the plan, elevation above the horizon, kept within ±89°). The eye and the screen axes, turning, panning so the scene follows the pointer, zooming at the cursor so the point under it stays put, fitting a box from a direction (`fitOrbit`), the toolbar's presets (3/4 from the south-west, top with north up, front from the south, side from the east) and `parseOrbit` for a stored value. Pure and unit-tested. |
| `theme.ts` | The colours per kind of solid and of the edge lines, read from the CSS variables in `styles.css` (`--scene-*`, `--canvas`) so the view follows the theme. `LIGHT_SCENE_THEME` and `DARK_SCENE_THEME` (the `.dark` block) are the fixed palettes renders use; the light one's solid colours are core's `SOLID_COLORS` (`exchange/gltf.ts`), which the glTF export writes, so change a kind's colour there. |
| `offscreen.ts` | `renderBuildingToPng`: the building drawn once by a `Scene3dView` into a canvas the caller makes, fitted from a direction, after the imported models it shows have loaded, returned as PNG bytes (base64): `view.snapshot` with `view: '3d'` and `export.image`. `theme` picks the palette; `transparent` makes the renderer's canvas alpha-capable, clears it to alpha 0 and drops the sky (`Scene3dView`'s `transparent` option). When WebGL gives a smaller drawing buffer than asked for (past the GPU's limit) it throws a `SnapshotSizeError` instead of returning a smaller image. |
| `scene.ts` | `BuildingScene`: one three group per level holding its solids and the connectors that start on it, in mm with z up. `update` rebuilds only the levels whose inputs changed (the level's entity record, the levels, the building, the layers, the connectors and the limits, compared by identity: the mirror replaces exactly what a patch touched) and the connectors when they or the levels change. Each part is a non-indexed mesh, flat-shaded, with its texture coordinates, with its edges (faces meeting at more than 25°) drawn as lines; glass is transparent; materials are physically based and shared per kind, or per material reference for frames, leaves and finishes. Every object carries what it stands for (level, kind, entity or connector ids) for picking. `applyDisplay` shows, hides and places the level groups for a `SceneDisplay` (all levels; the active one alone; the active one and those below it, with a clipping plane at the cut for the connector, rail, car and glass materials and their edges, since the solids cut the active level's walls themselves) and spreads them by `explode` (up to `EXPLODE_GAP`, 4 m, per level). |
| `decor.ts` | `LevelDecor`: a level's furniture and lights in 3D, kept in step item by item (recipes' slot meshes, imported models cloned from `ModelCache` with their material slots repainted (found by the slot `ModelCache` marks on each material; a flat-shaded primitive keeps a flat-shaded repaint), a box in the theme's `standIn` colour (`--scene-stand-in`) standing in while loading or after a failure, its geometry freed when it goes, the project's lights as three lights, all off until Night), placed by core's `itemMatrix`. |
| `materials.ts` | `DecorMaterials`: one physically based material per material reference, and a flat-shaded variant on request (`get(ref, fallback, finish, flat)`) (a built-in's colour, roughness, metalness and opacity, its pattern as a repeating `CanvasTexture` sized in mm for the meshes' mm UVs, or a paint), clipped together by the cutaway. |
| `models.ts` | `ModelCache`: imported GLB models loaded once from `library://` with three's `GLTFLoader`, turned into mm with z up and each material marked with its repaint slot (`modelFromGltf`: core's `gltfMaterialSlots` by the glTF material index three records in `parser.associations`, so an unnamed, padded or long-named material repaints); a failed load is tried again on the first build `MODEL_RETRY_MS` (5 s) after it, or at once when the project's assets change (`retryFailed`); `settled()` for a snapshot. |
| `view.ts` | `Scene3dView`: a WebGL renderer on a canvas (the constructor throws when there is no WebGL context), the world in metres (the solids' millimetres scaled down, for depth precision), a sky light and a key light that follows the camera from its upper left (no shadows, so software GL keeps up), frames drawn only when something changed. Left-drag orbits, right- or middle-drag or Shift+drag pans, the wheel zooms at the cursor, Home fits, a click without a drag picks what is under it (`pick`: the first visible mesh, anything above the cutaway's plane skipped). `setDisplay` applies a display and rebuilds the level the cutaway cuts. A double click starts the walk camera on the floor under it (`startWalk`/`stopWalk`; glass is looked through; the point is taken back to its level's place by that level's own lift, furniture included; a start inside furniture or against a wall moves to the nearest clear spot, core's `nudgeOut`): keys held move it each frame through core's `walkRelative`, a drag turns and tilts it, PageUp/PageDown ride an elevator, Escape restores the orbit; while walking every level shows whole and in place, and each frame reports the walker (feet, yaw, pitch, level). The first document with something in it fits the camera unless an orbit was set (that fit is not reported, so a stored orbit arriving later still wins). Each frame reports the orbit, the view-projection matrix (building mm to clip space), the frame time, the meshes and the levels drawn. `setLighting('day' | 'night')`: Day is the sky and key light; Night a dim sky, a dark background and the design's lights, the 16 nearest the camera on (switched only when that set changes, since three rebuilds its shaders then). A lost WebGL context (`webglcontextlost`) stops drawing and is reported to `onContextLost(true)`; on `webglcontextrestored` three uploads the scene again and the view draws a frame (`onContextLost(false)`). |

The Angular host (`packages/modules/building/ui/scene-view.ts`) owns the canvas, sizes it with a `ResizeObserver`, re-reads the theme when `<html class>` changes, stores the camera in the project's view state and mirrors each frame into `data-camera3d` (the view-projection matrix, column-major), `data-orbit` (target, distance, azimuth, elevation), `data-meshes`, `data-levels`, `data-frame-ms` and `data-context` (`ok` or `lost`; while lost, the "3D needs WebGL" banner covers the canvas). The smoke test projects building points to window pixels with the matrix and reads the window's capture there (`screenPixel`), since a WebGL canvas has no 2D context to sample.

## Dependencies and why

| Package | Why |
|---|---|
| `rbush` 4 | An R-tree for culling what is drawn and for hit-testing candidates. ESM, so the Angular build raises no CommonJS warning; it ships no types, hence `@types/rbush` at the root. |
| `three` 0.186.1 | WebGL rendering for the 3D view, as the brief asks: meshes, lines, lights, cameras; its `GLTFLoader` and `RectAreaLightUniformsLib` addons load imported models and light area panels (no new dependency). Pinned exactly (three has no semver: any release may change the API); `@types/three` 0.186.0 is its types. Openings are cut without a CSG library (see the core README's solids). |

## Tests

`npm test` runs `*.test.ts` here in node: the camera mapping and zoom, the scene index (boxes per type, diffing, openings following walls), the editor without a canvas (scenes per level, underlay choice, fit, camera callbacks), the offscreen render through a fake canvas (an empty level, labels alone, the paper in the light or dark colour or left transparent, a level a column spans up into), the Select tool with connectors (`tools/select-connectors.test.ts`), `scene3d/orbit.test.ts` (the eye and axes, turning off the poles, panning with the pointer, zooming at the cursor, fitting a box, reading a stored orbit), `scene3d/decor.test.ts` (a GLB with an unnamed, a padded long-named and a normal-less material parsed by `GLTFLoader` in node: each marked with core's slot and repainted through it, the normal-less one flat-shaded; a stand-in box in the scene theme's colour, following the theme, its geometry freed on removal; a failed model fetched again after `MODEL_RETRY_MS` or on `retryFailed`), and `perf.test.ts`, which derives and indexes 5,000 walls under a loose bound and logs the times. Nothing here touches `ResizeObserver`, `OffscreenCanvas` or `Path2D` at import time; `Path2D` outlines are built lazily at draw time. Drawing itself is checked by the smoke test (`src/main/smoke/plan-ui.ts`, `scene-ui.ts`: there a context lost and restored through `WEBGL_lose_context`), which samples pixels and takes screenshots.
