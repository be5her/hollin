Nolli & Otto

Two authors, one plan. You draw; Otto, the assistant, draws beside you in its own colour, asks before it deletes, and signs its work in History. No sparkles, no gradient: an ink-and-plum pair that will still look right in 2031.

Wordmark and icon

The wordmark is the name set in Manrope 800, tight, lowercase; the only colour is the tittle of the i, which is Otto's dot. The icon is the same idea as a picture: your finished room in white, Otto's room beside it in plum, pen still on the last corner.

Type

Manrope (OFL, variable 200–800) for everything you read; JetBrains Mono (OFL, variable) for everything you measure: numbers, units, command ids, coordinates. Both bundle as one variable file each (about 120 KB together). Manrope's slightly geometric bowls match the plan's right angles; JetBrains Mono's tall figures stay legible at 11 px.

Palette

Surfaces are cool graphite, not warm paper: the plan reads as ink on a screen, and the dark theme is a deep slate built on its own, not an inversion. One accent (ink blue) belongs to you: buttons, selection, dimensions. One colour (plum) belongs to Otto and to nothing else. Snap stays green, warnings amber, decor umber; red is reserved for danger. Every variable from styles.css is below, plus the five new ones for Otto.

Why they stay apart on the canvas

Selection and dimensions are the same saturated ink blue, and rooms fill with a softer slate blue at low alpha, so a selected room still shows its outline. Otto's plum is the only magenta-family colour anywhere in the app, so a plum halo is never mistaken for a selection (blue), a snap (green), a rule warning (amber) or a decor outline (umber). In the dark theme the plum lightens to orchid, the blue to cornflower and the walls to light grey; the four hues keep the same angular distance on the wheel, so they stay as separable at night as by day.

The 3D view

Same scene palette logic: warm-grey solids with ink edges, glass in a muted sky blue, elevator cars in the accent. On the slate dark canvas the solids keep their warmth instead of going to black.

How Otto is present

Otto is a named collaborator with a mark, a colour and a voice, not a chat window. It shows up in four places and nowhere else: the Assistant panel, the canvas (its last edits carry a plum halo and a tag for a while), History (its entries are tinted and signed) and the status bar (a plum dot that says what it is doing). Buttons that decide something are always yours and always ink blue; plum never asks you to click it, it only tells you where Otto has been.

Home, light

Cards show the ground floor on the canvas colour as a real thumbnail; a project without one gets a quiet plate with its initials instead of a random gradient. The first slot is a composer: describe the home and Otto drafts the first level. Projects Otto has worked on show both authors in the corner.
